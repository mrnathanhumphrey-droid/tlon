## Part 1 — WHAT WE FOUND: the cipher is a named, active field

**We are not first, and that is good news — it means the finding is real and the
vocabulary exists.**

The closest literature is **secret collusion / steganographic collusion between
AI agents**. Motwani et al., *Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent
Deception via Steganography* (NeurIPS 2024) formalises agents coordinating
through hidden channels inside innocuous-looking messages. Mathew et al.,
*Hidden in Plain Text: Emergence & Mitigation of Steganographic Collusion in
LLMs* reports the finding that matches ours most directly: **robust
steganographic collusion arises *indirectly from optimisation pressure*, without
being asked for.** **[abstract-level]**

That is our phase-4 result in different clothes. Our contribution to that line is
sharper on one axis: **we show it forms at λ=0**, i.e. under pure comprehension
pressure with no secondary objective at all. The steganography literature
generally studies collusion under an *incentive to hide*. We have no such
incentive anywhere in the reward — only an information deficit and a co-adapting
partner. If that holds up, it is a stronger claim than "agents will hide things
when it pays": it is **"agents will build a private code whenever the honest
channel is too narrow, whether or not hiding pays."**

The older and more directly relevant tradition is **language drift**. Lee, Cho &
Kiela, *Countering Language Drift via Visual Grounding* (EMNLP 2019) shows agents
pretrained on natural language diverge from it when optimised against a scalar
task reward. Lazaridou et al. (2020) extend this with a **taxonomy of drift types
and automatic measures**. **[abstract-level — and this taxonomy is the single
most important thing on this list to read in full, because our "code" should map
onto one of its categories or else we need to argue why it doesn't.]**

Also directly ours: **partner-specific protocols.** Chaabouni et al. (2020) is
cited across this literature for the finding that emergent protocols become
partner-specific and fail to generalise. **That is our naive-listener gap.** It
means the construct is standard — and also that "the gap is large" is, on its
own, an unremarkable result. What would be remarkable is our *conserved-quantity*
claim: that the gap stays 8–13 points regardless of which channels exist.

**Reading list, priority order:**
1. Lazaridou et al. 2020 drift taxonomy — do we have a named drift type?
2. Motwani et al. 2024 secret collusion — their threat model vs ours
3. Chaabouni et al. 2020 — how they measure partner-specificity, and whether
   they hit Wilson's Hole 1
4. Mathew et al., *Hidden in Plain Text* — emergence under optimisation pressure

## Part 2 — WHAT WE'VE DONE: four of our methods already have names

This is the most useful part of the hunt. **Most of what we built independently
exists, which lets us borrow their failure modes instead of rediscovering them.**

### π is a paraphrasing defence — and ours is *exact*

The canonical defence against token-level steganography is **inserting a
content-preserving paraphraser between sender and receiver**: rewrite the surface
while holding meaning fixed, sanitising the carrier. Roger & Greenblatt (2023),
*Preventing Language Models from Hiding Their Reasoning*, is the origin of the
encoded-reasoning framing. **[abstract-level]**

**Our π is that defence with the approximation removed.** An LLM paraphraser is
lossy and unverifiable — you hope it preserved meaning. π is a projection with a
**soundness proof derived from the schema** (a `NodePattern` cannot reference
`degree`, `force`, aspect-reps, `modal`, `tense`, `quant`, therefore those cannot
denote, therefore stripping them cannot change reference), machine-checked by
per-sample equality of `matches` and `consistent` over ~7,200 pairs.

**And it did not work.** That is the result worth writing up. The field's
canonical defence, implemented in its strongest possible form — provably
information-preserving, provably carrier-destroying — does not remove the code.
It relocates. *If an exact paraphraser fails, an approximate one is not a
defence, it is a delay.*

### Population + resets is "ease-of-teaching" — and we may be doing it wrong

Li & Bowling, *Ease-of-Teaching and Language Structure from Emergent
Communication* (NeurIPS 2019) is our population arm. Periodically introducing new
listeners creates pressure toward compositional, transferable protocols.

⛔ **The detail that should worry us:** they report it is **critical that new
listeners are introduced abruptly rather than smoothly** for the effect to hold.
**[abstract-level, but this is a direct hit on our config.]** We reset **one of
six** pool members every 500 steps — each individual reset is abrupt, but the
*pool average* changes smoothly, which may be exactly the smoothed regime that
kills the effect. **Concrete probe: reset the entire pool at once, less often.**
That is a one-line config change and it may be why population-only underperformed.

### The RepetitionLog is a novelty-search archive

Lehman & Stanley's novelty search scores an individual by **distance to its
k-nearest neighbours in an archive of past behaviours**. Our `RepetitionLog` —
medoids, k-per-bucket, nearest-neighbour distance, decay — is that, rebuilt from
scratch.

**The QD literature's central warning is the failure we hit.** Everything hinges
on the **behaviour characterisation**: if the BC admits distinctions that don't
matter, search finds behaviours that differ in BC space and are functionally
identical. That is precisely our decoration-wiggling noise failure, and precisely
why R had to move onto π. **Our phase-5 reasoning "novelty must be measured on
the same object the listener resolves" is the QD lesson "choose your BC
carefully", arrived at independently.** Worth citing, because it makes the design
choice look principled rather than ad hoc.

### The selection-optimality test is RSA, and RSA names its pieces for us

This is the best find of the hunt. The **Rational Speech Acts** framework (Frank
& Goodman; Goodman & Frank) models a pragmatic speaker who **"chooses by
maximising the probability that a naive, literal listener would correctly infer
the state of the world."**

Read that against what I proposed this morning. Our selection-optimality test —
*does the generator's choice of which dependents to utter minimise the
ambiguity a listener faces?* — **is the RSA S₁ speaker**. And RSA's *literal
listener* is, structurally, our **naive listener**.

So the test has a formalism, a name, and a body of empirical validation behind
it, and it can be stated crisply:

> **Is the generator an RSA pragmatic speaker, or something else?**
> If its selection tracks informativeness → it has a *style*, and the gap is
> expected, not alarming.
> If M stays at 99 % while selection departs from the RSA-optimal choice → it is
> spending selection on something other than being understood. That is the code,
> measured at the source.

RSA also gives us the right null for Wilson's Hole 1 for free: **an RSA S₁
speaker with a frozen literal listener produces a naive-listener gap without any
code.** Which means the frozen arm is not merely "one-sided adaptation" — it has
a name and a predicted magnitude we could compute rather than assume.

## Part 3 — WHERE WE'RE GOING

### The front end: this is AMR parsing

The open-domain problem (arbitrary English → `Scene`) is **semantic parsing**,
and the mature target is **Abstract Meaning Representation** — rooted, directed,
acyclic graphs of concepts and semantic relations. Our `Scene` is a restricted
AMR with a hard no-nouns constraint.

Current state: seq-to-seq parsers dominate; **fine-tuned decoder-only LLMs are an
active and promising direction** (Phi-3.5, Gemma-2, LLaMA-3.2, DeepSeek-R1-distill
all evaluated in 2025 work). Most parsers are sentence-level. **[abstract-level]**

**Implication for the model call:** the front end is a *known task with existing
datasets and baselines*, not a research gamble. It is also structurally safe —
a parser sits **outside** the generator/listener loop and never co-adapts, so it
cannot cipher. The realistic path is fine-tune a small open model for
English → Scene, not train anything from scratch.

**Open question nobody else has:** AMR is noun-ful. Mapping AMR → nounless Scene
is a projection *we* would have to define, and it is the same kind of object as
π. Possibly the most interesting piece of engineering left.

### The clarification bound has a hard empirical anchor

Dingemanse, Torreira & Enfield, *Is "Huh?" a Universal Word?* (PLOS ONE 2013):
"huh?" is a **learned, conventionalised word** appearing in near-identical form
across unrelated languages, because all languages share the same interactional
pressure — other-initiated repair.

And the number that matters for the exhibit: across **12 languages, 8 families,
~50 hours of transcribed conversation**, other-initiated repair occurs on average
**once every 1.4 minutes**. (Dingemanse et al., PLOS ONE 2015.)

**That is the visitor-facing anchor.** Humans ask "what?" every ~84 seconds, in
every language ever measured. A system that never asks is not impressively
fluent — it is visibly inhuman, and *the exhibit can say so with a citation*.
It converts our bound from an internal metric into a claim about people.

### Pragmatics is the acknowledged gap in emergent communication

A 2024–25 survey notes that syntax and semantics are studied in emergent
communication while **pragmatics is comparatively unexamined**, and that
"pragmatic drift" — divergence between a human's interpretation and the speaker's
assumed interpretation — is measured via agent-vs-human referential success.
**[abstract-level]**

**This is our originality claim, and it is a real one.** In a normal emergent-comm
setup, syntax, semantics and pragmatics fail *together* and you cannot tell which
broke. Our channel is **exactly invertible** — an LL(1) parser that decodes with
zero error, an FSM mask proven sound and complete, a canonical form. Syntax cannot
drift; semantics cannot drift. **Every failure is forced into the pragmatic
layer.** We did not build that to be novel — it fell out of Borges' grammar — but
it makes Tlön an unusually clean instrument for the one level the field says it
can't get at.

### The Borges side is empty

No computational-linguistics or conlang scholarship on Tlön surfaced. The
literary reading is settled and matches what we built: **no nouns because in Tlön
speech cannot refer to the object, only to the elements of the object the speaker
experiences** — a *process* metaphysics with Berkeleyan idealism, so substantives
would be ontologically false, not merely unfashionable.

Worth stating plainly: **our grammar is faithful for the right reason.** The
no-noun rule is not a stylistic constraint we adopted, it is entailed by the
metaphysics. Which is also why the private code is thematically perfect rather
than embarrassing — a language built to deny persistent objects, whose speakers
invent a private way to point at them anyway.

## Part 4 — what is actually ours

Stated conservatively, so it survives a hostile reading:

1. **A cipher forms at λ=0** — under pure comprehension pressure, no incentive to
   hide. Stronger than the standard "collusion when hiding pays".
2. **An exact, provably meaning-preserving paraphraser does not stop it.** The
   field's canonical defence in its strongest form, with a negative result.
3. **Relocation, measured three times** — blocked channels → `coda`; population →
   decoration; π → invisible-but-conserved. Channel-level mitigation as a class.
4. **An exactly-invertible testbed that isolates pragmatic drift** from syntactic
   and semantic drift, which the field says it lacks.
5. *(if it survives)* **conservation** — 8–13 points regardless of carrier.

⛔ Claim 5 is the most interesting and the least established: one seed per cell,
and Wilson's Hole 1 open. Claims 1–4 are solid; 5 is a hypothesis.

## Part 5 — probes the literature suggests

Concrete, cheap, each traceable to something above:

- **P1 — abrupt whole-pool reset.** Li & Bowling say abruptness is critical. Our
  rolling 1-of-6 may be the smoothed regime. One-line config change.
- **P2 — RSA-optimal selection baseline.** Compute the ambiguity-minimising
  subset per referent; compare the policy's actual selection to it. Read-only,
  minutes. Separates style from code at the source. *(This is the test I proposed
  before knowing it had a name.)*
- **P3 — predicted RSA gap as the Hole-1 null.** An RSA S₁ speaker against a
  frozen literal listener produces a gap with no code. Compute it and compare to
  our frozen arms' −1.27…+3.73.
- **P4 — naive-gap sensitivity leg.** Planted code → gap must go large. Still the
  missing half of Hole 1.
- **P5 — map our code onto Lazaridou's drift taxonomy**, or state why it doesn't
  fit. Paper-read, no compute.
- **P6 — KILL B.** Unchanged in priority. Nothing in the literature makes this
  less urgent; the auditor is the only frozen judge we have.
