## 0 · Why

IDF-1/1b established that the model's input — `t−1` only — carries at most
partial information about which roots were inherited. At its own lag-1
fidelity the model sits at or below the best window-1 floor. So the D6 floor
has **not** been shown to be a weight property, and has **not** been shown not
to be one. IDF-2 is the positive test the release arc has never had: give the
input what the corpus rule depends on, and see whether release installs.

- It installs → the D6 floor was an **information** problem. The art piece's
  path is an input design, not a capacity climb.
- It floors even with the information present → *"the weights resist"* is back
  on the table, now with a controlled argument behind it, and rung 1 becomes a
  real question again.

**⚠️ The two outcomes do not carry equal information, and that is stated here
rather than discovered in the write-up.** The marker hands the model a literal
list of roots to avoid, and Step 0b will have *proved* that list is sufficient.
So **INSTALLS** means "the model can follow an explicit avoid-list" — near the
floor of what is interesting, and a long way from the art piece, where nothing
hands a window its own provenance. **FLOORS is the informative branch**: a model
that cannot avoid roots it is explicitly told to avoid is a strong result about
the weights. W2 is the only arm in this design that resembles the art piece,
which is why it is in the primary set rather than optional.

### ⭐ Clearing a closed avenue, in writing

`briefs/wilson_2026_09_25/03_DO_NOT_REPROPOSE.md` closes **"a LoRA-on-frozen-
weights recipe for release, at this rank"**, on the ground that *corpus-signal
strength is exhausted as a lever* (D6, measured). The bar for reopening is a
new argument, not a fresh memory.

**The new argument:** IDF-2 does not change signal strength. Every arm here
trains at the same rank, the same config and a matched weight-level dose, on a
corpus built to the same responsiveness. What changes is **what information the
input contains**. D6 measured the corpus axis; this measures the input axis, and
they are not the same lever. ⛔ If that argument is rejected, the probe does not
run — it is not carried as an assumption.

## 1 · ⛔ One definition of "inherited", used in the builder AND the reader

The generator's true `TTurn.inherited` cannot be computed at read time (the
model's turns have no bookkeeping). So the rule is redefined to be one the
reader can compute:

    held(t−1) := roots(t−1) ∩ roots(t−2)

- Build a new corpus recipe with `barred_fn = held` (whole-surface containment
  bar, as now). **One function in `tlon/discourse/transient.py`**, imported by
  the corpus builder, the row builder and the lag reader. A test asserts all
  three resolve to **the same object** (`is`, not equal results).
- **Recipe name: `content-transient-held`.**
- ⛔ On turns **1 and 2** the marker is `(none)`, in train and read alike.
  Generating turn 2 needs `roots(t−2)` where `t−2` is turn 0, which does not
  exist, so `held = ∅` there as well as at turn 1. *(This is an off-by-one the
  first draft carried; it is corrected here because the builder and the reader
  must agree byte-for-byte.)*

### ⛔⛔ The new recipe is quarantined, using the mechanism that already exists

`transient.RECIPES` is not a list of labels — it is the factorial's quarantine.
`tlon.act2.factorial` validates against it, and anything absent from it **cannot
become a cell, be labelled, or be paired**. `CONTENT_PERSISTENT` is already held
outside it for exactly this reason, in `DOSE_ARM_RECIPES`.

So `content-transient-held` goes in a **third tuple** beside them —
`PROBE_RECIPES` — added to `ALL_RECIPES` (which the builder accepts) and
**never** to `RECIPES`. That makes "never pooled with the `dd40e22f` /
`16abeb8d` cells" structural rather than a sentence in a document.

### ⚠️ `held` does not fully control lag-2 — so the gap may be narrower than IDF-1's

⛔ **`held` is not a weaker version of `inherited`; under this recipe it is
the rule.** There is no hidden bookkeeping left over to be weaker than. *(An
earlier draft framed it as a weakening, which imported IDF-1's recipe into
IDF-2's — see §3-0b.)*

What is true is narrower and matters for §6's arithmetic: a root present in
`roots(t−2)` but absent from `roots(t−1)` is **not** barred, yet it contributes
to lag-2 exactly as much as a barred one. So this corpus's own lag-2 will sit
**above** the old recipe's.

⚠️ **That compresses the denominator.** `ct-s20624`'s corpus lag-2 is
**0.0271** (`corpus_ct-s20624/manifest.json`), and IDF-1's blind-to-true gap
was **0.4171**. If the held corpus lands materially higher, the 0c gap shrinks
and a "15 point" band becomes a smaller raw difference — harder to resolve.

⇒ **0a reports the gap before Step P runs**, and Step P's SD is expressed in
the same units, so the resolvability check in §3A is made against the gap this
recipe actually has rather than the one IDF-1 had.

### ⛔⛔ 0b is a RED-PROOF, and a shortfall is a bug — not a measurement

*(An earlier draft of this section reinterpreted a 0b shortfall as "the part of
the rule no surface reader can recover" and reported it as a finding. **That is
wrong for this recipe and it is reversed here.** — Wilson, 2026-09-26.)*

oracle that sees the marker and applies the same bar is **running the
generator's own rule**, so its lag-2 must match the corpus's within sampling

⇒ A shortfall cannot be an unrecoverable remainder. It can only mean **the
marker, the bar, or the reader's recomputation of `held` is wired wrong.**

⭐ The unrecoverable-remainder quantity is real, but it belongs to the **old**

IDF-1's recipe wearing IDF-2's name.

⭐ The "failures we learn from" standard is still met on this branch: what a
0b failure teaches is **where the plumbing broke**, which is what a red-proof
is for.

## 3A · Step P — the power check, BEFORE either adapter is bought

⛔⛔ **§6 has one failure branch that learns nothing: the bands not resolving.**
If `2 × between-seed SD > 15 points`, the reading is *no verdict* no matter what
the point estimate did — three adapters bought, ~20 GPU-h spent, and the table
could never have separated its own cells. That branch is **foreseeable now**,
and it does not have to be discovered afterwards.

**C0 already exists.** So its seeded reads at the §5 size run **first**, and
they measure the between-seed SD of `closes` directly, on a real adapter, at the
real read size, before a single training step is paid for.

### ⛔ C0 is read at EIGHT seeds here, not three

*(Wilson, 2026-09-26.)* Three reads give an SD with **2 degrees of freedom** —
noisy enough that the trip below passes or fires substantially by chance, which
would make the gate that protects the table the least reliable number in it.
**Eight seeds give 7 df.** C0 is untrained, so this buys degrees of freedom with
**reads only, no training** — ≈ 2.3 GPU-h more than three seeds would cost, and
it is the cheapest information in the design.

- **PASS** — `2 × SD ≤ 15 points`. The table resolves its own boundaries.
  Proceed to §4.
- **FAIL** — `2 × SD > 15 points`. ⛔ **Stop before training.** The read size or
  the seed count is too small for the table as locked, and the choice —
  more chains, more seeds, or wider bands — is a **re-lock**, not a mid-run
  adjustment. *(Widening the bands after seeing this number, but before any
  treatment exists, is legitimate; widening them after M has read is
  retrofitting. The distinction is why this step sits here.)*

### The second estimate, and the deviation it can trigger

`RESULTS_IDF2` also reports the **pooled between-seed SD across the arms that
gate a cell — M, C1 and M-strip — 3 × 2 = 6 df.** *(Wilson, final: pool only
what gates.)*

⛔ **Three arms are deliberately out of the pool, each for its own reason:**

| out | why |
|---|---|
| **W2** | different input shape, so its read-to-read variance is not known to be comparable; descriptive only |
| **C0** | Step P already estimates its SD separately at 7 df. **That is the gate's estimate; the pool is the cross-check** — the same number cannot be both |
| **M-shuffle** | built to *perturb* behaviour (it marks the wrong roots), so its variance may differ **by design**; descriptive only, reported separately |

> ⛔ If the **6-df pool** and **Step P's 7-df SD** disagree by more than a
> factor of **1.5**, that is recorded as a **DEVIATIONS entry before any §6
> cell is read** — not after.

⚠️ The **1.5** is a deviation *trigger*, not a reading-table constant. It is
Wilson's, recorded as his, and it is named here so that §6's "no invented
constants" claim stays literally true rather than true-by-filing.

- **Three independent seeds per treatment arm** — M, C1, W2, and the §7
  mechanism controls.
- ⛔ **Eight for C0**, all of them in Step P, for the degrees of freedom §3A

Report per read: the raw lag profile 1–4, `z`, `n_pairs`, and
`resolving_power` vs `threshold_for_lag`.

### ⛔⛔ z gates nothing here, and this is the fix that matters most

*(The first draft set the read size and the `z ≤ 3.0` bar independently, as if
they were unrelated quantities. They are not.)*

`C0` was read at 12 × 10 = 120 turns, which is **96 lag-2 pairs**. From
`runs/act2/retrain12_ct/model_lag_ct-s20624.json`: lag-2 raw **0.3854**, null
μ **0.1353**, σ **0.0381**, **z +6.565**. At 48 × 10 = 480 turns the lag-2 cell
holds **384 pairs**, 4× as many, and the null's σ is the spread of a mean, so it
falls by about half.

- Projected σ ≈ **0.0191** ⇒ `z ≤ 3.0` requires a raw lag-2 of ≈ **0.1925**.
- Against the C0→corpus span (0.3854 → 0.0285) that is **≈ 54 % of the gap
  closed just to enter the cell**, while the first draft's *partial* bar was
  35 %.
- **C0 itself would read z ≈ 13.1** at this size, on unchanged behaviour.

So enlarging the read to buy resolving power silently tightened the headline bar
by about 2×. ⭐ And this is the same axis as **`RESULTS_IDF1B` §6's open item**,
which IDF-2 inherits: *"a z-floor cannot detect a half-collapsed speaker at
large n; only the raw profile can."* That item sits inside a locked document and
has not been touched. It is not repeated here.

⚠️ The projection above assumes M's null resembles C0's. μ and σ are
**re-estimated per read** from the generated turns, so they are not constants —
which is the second reason a z threshold cannot be locked in advance.

**Therefore:** every threshold in §6 is stated as a **fraction of the 0c gap**,
which is exactly the convention `PREREG_IDF1B` §4 already established for the
same reason. `z` is reported beside every number, with `n_pairs`, and gates
nothing.

⛔ **One consequence of that is named now, not later:** INSTALLS is a claim
about **the marker's effect**, not a claim that release reached chance. M's raw
lag-2 and its z are reported next to the corpus's own so nobody can read the
one as the other.

### Perceive guard — an effect size, never a z

M's raw lag-1 ≥ **⚠️ SIGN-OFF 0.8 ×** C1's raw lag-1, or the read is recorded as
**perceive-collapsed** and never as release. Reported against C0's lag-1 too.
⛔ This is the one guard that still gates, and it is deliberately not a z: an arm
at lag-1 0.4961 — under half the model's fidelity — passed the z floor at
**+439**, because z scales with n.

### ⚠️⚠️ THE ONE THING IN THIS TABLE THAT IS NOT SETTLED — read before ruling

I said 15 and 35 were *"inherited verbatim from IDF-1b, rationale intact."*
**They are not, and the difference is a denominator role, not a value.**

- In `PREREG_IDF1B` §4, 35 % is the **absolute level of one oracle** on the
  blind-to-true gap. Its rationale: an arm closing a third of that gap means
  provenance is readable off the surface.
- Here I reused the numeral on a **difference between two trained adapters**.
  Requiring the marker *alone* to move a third of the entire oracle gap is a
  far harder ask than IDF-1b's, and nothing in IDF-1b's rationale supports it.

⭐ **The numeral survives; the role does not.** *(This is
`feedback_the_numbers_resolution_must_match_the_decisions`: name the decision's
unit, then confirm the number is denominated in it.)* So the table below keeps
IDF-1b's boundaries **in IDF-1b's own role — M's absolute level** — and carries
the causal attribution on the difference, where it needs no invented constant:

| role | quantity | boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **did release install?** | `closes(M)`, absolute | 15 / 35 points — IDF-1b's, unchanged |
| **was it the marker?** | `closes(M) − closes(C1)` | CI excludes 0, and §3A resolves it |

⛔ No new threshold enters this arc. **✅ SIGN-OFF #6 ACCEPTED (Wilson,
2026-09-26) — and extended to every branch, which the first pass of it failed to

### ⛔⛔ Why the extension was not optional

The restructuring landed in INSTALLS only. PARTIAL and FLOORS still tested M's
**absolute** level, which is the baseline's question, not the marker's — and
both failed in a specific, foreseeable way (Wilson's cases, recorded because
they are the reason the cells changed):

- **PARTIAL could fire on nothing.** C1 closes 20 points unmarked, M closes 22.

- **FLOORS could not fire on its own result.** C1 and M both close 20. That is
  a clean *"the marker did nothing"* — the exact finding FLOORS exists to catch
  — and FLOORS is blocked because M sits above 15, so the run reports **no
  verdict on the result the branch was built for.**

⇒ Every branch now carries both a level condition and a difference condition.

- **INSTALLS** — on **all three seeds**, `closes(M)` ≥ **35 points**; **and**
  `closes(M) − closes(C1)` has a CI excluding 0; **and** M-strip's drop toward
  C1 has a CI excluding 0 (§7); **and** the perceive guard passes on M.
  ⇒ Release is installable given provenance in the input. **D6's floor was
  information.** ⚠️ Read §0's asymmetry note before reading this as more than it

- **USES-MARKER-PARTIAL** — `closes(M)` lands **between 15 and 35 points** on
  all three seeds, **and** `closes(M) − closes(C1)` has a CI **excluding 0**,
  **and** the per-root readout CI excludes 0.
  ⇒ Information helps and something else still limits. **Name it in the
  results. No rung decision follows automatically.**

- **FLOORS** — `closes(M) − closes(C1)` has a CI **including 0** **and** that
  CI's **upper bound is below 15 points**, **and** the per-root readout CI
  includes 0.
  ⭐ **No absolute-level condition.** The marker's effect is indistinguishable
  from zero *and* a 15-point effect would have been detected if present — an
  equivalence test, not an absence of evidence. The **15** is doing exactly the
  job it does in §3A, bounding a resolvable difference, so **no new constant
  enters**.
  ⇒ The model does not use provenance it is handed.
  ⛔⛔ **Scoped, and the scope is part of the reading: *at this rank and this
  recipe*.** It is **not** a substrate claim and **not** a general LoRA claim.
  The rank confound named in `03_DO_NOT_REPROPOSE.md` — *"rank was held fixed
  on purpose, so D6 says nothing about capacity"* — applies to this branch
  unchanged. The weights-resist reading revives as **a question**, and rung 1

  2026-09-26.)*

- **Anything else** — report everything, no verdict, full disclosure.

  which is precisely the contrast wanted.
  **Predicts `closes(M-strip)` falls toward C1's.**
  ⭐ **Threshold: the CI on that drop excludes 0.** No fraction. *(This
  replaces "gives back ≥ 50 %", which was the last invented constant in the
  table.)*
- **M-shuffle** — the marker names roots of `t−1` that are **not** in `held`
  (same count). Predicts the model avoids the wrongly-marked roots: lag-2 rises
  and per-root avoidance tracks the **marker**, not the truth.
- **Per-root readout** — `P(root of t−1 reappears in t | marked)` vs
  `P(… | unmarked)`, with a CI, computed from the M read transcripts (no extra
  generation). This is the direct measure of whether the model uses the marker,
  and it appears as a conjunct in all three cells of §6.
