- Ji et al., "Language Models Resist Alignment: Evidence from Data Compression," arXiv 2406.06144. Names it ELASTICITY: post-fine-tune models revert toward the pretraining distribution; fine-tuning "disproportionately undermines alignment relative to pretraining, potentially by orders of magnitude"; elasticity INCREASES with model size and pretraining-data scale.
- The Superficial Alignment Hypothesis line (fine-tuning changes how a model talks, not what it fundamentally is) + Wei et al. (instruction-tuning worsens the ability to REPLACE known associations) + counterfactual-reasoning work (arXiv 2506.15732: "the difficulty of overriding strong parametric priors" when context conflicts with prior).

⛔⛔ **ELASTICITY IS NOT WHAT WE MEASURED, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS REVIEWER-VISIBLE.** Ji et al.'s elasticity is REVERSION: align a model, then continued training/compression pulls it back toward pretraining. D6 is **FAILURE TO INSTALL IN THE FIRST PLACE** — release never went in, at any dose, across layer capacity and the token mapping. Adjacent, not identical.
⭐ **THE TEST THAT DECIDES THE FRAMING:** call D6 elasticity and a reviewer who knows Ji asks for the REVERSION CURVE — a fine-tune-then-drift measurement this project has never made. What we have needs no reversion dynamics at all: a dose-response showing the prior is immovable ON INSTALLATION. That is a different and arguably cleaner claim.
⭐ **SO THE CLAIM IS "PARAMETRIC-PRIOR RESISTANCE TO INSTALLATION", WITH ELASTICITY CITED AS THE NEAREST NAMED NEIGHBOUR.** This changes the relationship to the literature from *"we are an instance of elasticity"* (checkable, and wrong) to *"we are a cleaner adjacent result; elasticity is the neighbour"* (defensible).

The distinction above is the reading that follows from the abstract-level description and it is exactly the kind of claim that must not be made from a summary. OUR RESULT IS A CLEANER NEIGHBOUR THAN THE FIELD'S VERSIONS. The elasticity / superficial-alignment literature measures on alignment/safety/factual behaviors — contaminated, fuzzy, value-laden.
