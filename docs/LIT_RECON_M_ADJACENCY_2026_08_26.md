## OBJECT 1 — epistemic/doxastic accessibility relation

**Exists?** Yes, standard and old.

**Shape:** a **directed binary relation over states**, specified by *relational
properties*, not by a distance function. **Non-metric — there is no d(·,·)
anywhere in the object.** The standard doxastic (belief) frame is **KD45**:
serial, transitive, Euclidean — **neither reflexive nor symmetric**. The standard
epistemic (knowledge) frame is **S5**: reflexive, symmetric, transitive — an
equivalence relation.

**Canonical cite:** Bezhanishvili & van der Hoek, *Structures for Epistemic
Logic*; SEP *Epistemic Logic*.

⛔ **CORRECTS OUR EXPECTATION.** The hunt brief said symmetric accessibility "is
standardly rejected — this validates our symmetric-matrix=failure ruling." **It
does not.** S5, the standard frame for *knowledge*, is symmetric. What the
literature validates is the weaker and more useful claim: **the object is a
relation, not a metric space.** Our symmetry ruling has to stand on Borges
("temporal, not spatial"), which it already did — it does not get outside
support, and it does not need it.

⭐ **Convergence worth noting, not leaning on:** KD45 drops reflexivity (belief
does not entail truth). RULING 8 excludes the diagonal (a series in which nothing
changes is not a succession). Same shape, wholly different reasons.

**The one sentence that bears on our stuck point:** McGrath (2021) separates
**suspension of judgment — transitional**, its justification sensitive to
"future-comparative" factors (you will have better evidence tomorrow) — from
**agnosticism — terminal and stable**, which is not.

⇒ ⭐⭐ **"Is Withdrawn a hub or a sink" may be the wrong question.** The
literature says the withdrawn region contains **both**, as *different objects*.
Our Withdrawn block holds exactly two forms — `hrin` (doubted) and `nem`
(denied). **The block may be non-uniform in exactly the way Irreal was.** Flagged,
not derived.

**Cite:** McGrath, *Being Neutral: Agnosticism, Inquiry and the Suspension of
Judgment*, Noûs 55(2), 2021.

## OBJECT 2 — evidentiality typology: scale, hierarchy, or features?

**Exists?** Yes. Aikhenvald is the standard reference.

**Shape:** a **subsumption hierarchy — a tree, therefore a PARTIAL ORDER, not a
total-order scale.** Aikhenvald's five recurrent categories are Visual Sensory,
Non-Visual Sensory, Inferential, Hearsay Reportative, Quotative Reportative, and
the load-bearing structural fact is that *"evidentials may refer to the leaves of
this hierarchy, or to any of the supertypes"* — a language may mark VISUAL
specifically, or DIRECT, which subsumes VISUAL. **Subsumption, not position on a
line.**

**Canonical cite:** Aikhenvald, *Evidentiality*, OUP 2004. (Faller, *Remarks on
Evidential Hierarchies*, for the hierarchy question specifically.)

⇒ **The contact "axis" was wrong to be a line.** The typologically standard
object is a partial order — which is directed, temporal-legitimate, and carries
no geometry.

⛔⛔ **THE BIGGER RESULT: our M-class is not an evidential inventory.** It is a
mixed bag of **three typologically distinct categories**:

| Tlön forms | standard category |
|---|---|
| `xöl` seen · `hrix` heard · `ten` felt · `plun` inferred | **evidentiality proper** (source of information) |
| `nem` denied · `hrin` doubted | **judgement / epistemic modality** (speaker's commitment) — a *sibling* of evidentiality under propositional modality, per Chung & Timberlake and Bybee, not a member of it |
| `mir` wished · `frax` feared | **volitive / buletic modality** (desires, wishes, fears) — *"a fundamentally different semantic domain"* |
| `mar` remembered | marginal — evidential in some inventories, not in Aikhenvald's five |
| `xos` dreamt | not a standard evidential category at all |

**The one sentence that bears on our stuck point:** evidentiality marks *source of
information*; volitive modality concerns *the speaker's desires and wishes*, and
the typology treats these as different semantic domains, not points on one scale.

⇒ ⭐⭐ **The Affective split is FORCED BY TYPOLOGY, with no coordinate system
required** — `mir`/`frax` are a different modal category, full stop. **And the
same argument splits Withdrawn off**, which we did not anticipate: `nem`/`hrin`
are judgement modality, also not evidentiality. Two of our four blocks are not
made of evidentials.

## OBJECT 3 — is affect orthogonal to epistemic content?

**Exists?** Yes — appraisal theory, and it is directly on point.

**Shape:** emotions are individuated by position on a small set of **appraisal
dimensions**. Smith & Ellsworth recovered **six orthogonal dimensions**:
pleasantness, anticipated effort, **certainty**, attentional activity,
self-other responsibility/control, situational control.

**Canonical cite:** Smith & Ellsworth, *Patterns of Cognitive Appraisal in
Emotion*, JPSP 48(4):813–838, 1985.

⛔⛔ **THIS REFUTES THE HYPOTHESIS IT WAS SENT TO SUPPORT.** **Certainty is one of
the dimensions that constitutes the emotion**, not something the emotion is
independent of. And the discrimination is specific: **fear and hope carry an
UNCERTAINTY appraisal; anger, disgust and happiness carry a CERTAINTY appraisal.**

⇒ **Affect is not orthogonal to epistemic state** — which is enough to kill the
*geometric justification* for Affective-adjacent-to-everything.

⛔⛔ **CORRECTED 2026-08-26 — THIS ENTRY ORIGINALLY OVER-REACHED, ONE SENTENCE
AFTER NAMING A WORD-TRAP.** It said `frax` "carries its own epistemic signature,
so it is not freely attachable to every contact level." **That does not follow.**
S&E's *certainty* is **outcome predictability** (*"understand what is happening
and can predict what will happen"*) — forward-looking. Tlön's M-class marks
**source of information** — backward-looking. **Two different objects wearing one
English word**, which is the identical trap to the "orthogonal" one flagged
immediately above it. You can be visually certain of what you see and fearful of
what it portends.

⇒ **The verdict is narrower: Object 3 REMOVES A JUSTIFICATION AND SUPPLIES NO
REPLACEMENT.** It says nothing about `frax`'s adjacency either way. See
`GROUPING_MUTATION_TEST_2026_08_26.md`.

⚠️ **A WORD-TRAP WORTH NAMING.** "Orthogonal" *does* appear in this literature —
the six appraisal dimensions are orthogonal **to each other** (factor-analytic
independence *within* the appraisal space). That is a completely different claim
from "affect is orthogonal to epistemic state," and the finding runs the other
way: **certainty is INSIDE the affect model.** Reading the first as the second
would have imported support that isn't there.
